Pulsar Timing, Dispersion & Folding Explorer

1. Astrophysical Context & Overview

Pulsars are highly magnetized, rotating neutron stars that emit beams of electromagnetic radiation out of their magnetic poles. Because their rotation is exceptionally stable, they act as precise cosmic clocks. As radiation traverses the ionized Interstellar Medium (ISM) en route to Earth, free electrons delay lower frequencies, requiring precise dedispersion and phase folding to recover clean integrated pulse profiles.

2. Pulse Period Folding Mechanics

To extract faint periodic signals from background noise, time-series data is folded modulo the pulsar's spin period (P) across thousands of rotations into discrete phase bins:

Φ(t) = ( t⁄P − ⌊t⁄P⌋ ) × Nbins

Where P represents the spin period [s] and Nbins is the resolution of the folded pulse profile.

3. Interstellar Dispersion Measure (DM)

The cold plasma dispersion relation delays lower radio frequencies by an amount proportional to the column density of free electrons along the galactic line of sight:

DM = ∫ ne dl   [pc cm−3]

4. Reference Python Pulsar Folding Snippet

import numpy as np

def fold_pulsar_timeseries(signal: np.ndarray, period_s: float, sample_rate_hz: float, n_bins: int = 128):
    dt = 1.0 / sample_rate_hz
    times = np.arange(len(signal)) * dt
    phases = (times % period_s) / period_s
    bin_indices = (phases * n_bins).astype(int) % n_bins
   
    folded_profile = np.zeros(n_bins)
    weights = np.zeros(n_bins)
   
    for val, b in zip(signal, bin_indices):
        folded_profile[b] += val
        weights[b] += 1
       
    return np.divide(folded_profile, np.maximum(weights, 1))
