Residual Spectral Variance Decay Floor

Asymptotic noise reduction floor converging toward 98.08% (V_∞ ≈ 0.0192)

Theoretical Foundation & Mathematical Dynamics

Spectral variance $\sigma^2(N, p_{\text{max}})$ quantifies total residual noise present in phase-sum signals after applying prime notch filters. As the cutoff threshold $p_{\text{max}} \to \infty$, the normalized variance ratio $\mathcal{V} = \sigma^2 / \sigma_{\text{raw}}^2$ drops asymptotically toward a fixed theoretical limit.

Normalizing for the removal of base primorial factors ($p \in \{2, 3, 5\}$), the residual variance converges to $\mathcal{V}_{\infty} = \prod_{p > 5} (1 - 1/p^2) \approx 0.0192$. Subtracting this value from unity yields an absolute noise reduction ceiling of **98.08%**.

This visualization plots the decay curve of normalized background variance against increasing domain thresholds $N$. The horizontal asymptote highlights the 98.08% noise floor, verifying the theoretical variance bounds established in the manuscript.
