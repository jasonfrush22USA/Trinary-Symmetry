NANOGrav Pulsar Timing Array Explorer

1. Mission & Array Objective

Millisecond pulsars (MSPs) act as ultra-stable cosmic clocks across the galaxy. By monitoring pulse times of arrival (TOAs) over decades-long baselines, pulsar timing arrays (PTAs) like NANOGrav search for minute quadrupolar spatial correlations induced by passing low-frequency gravitational waves from supermassive black hole binaries.

2. Timing Model & Residual Mechanics

Observed pulse arrival times are compared against a deterministic pulsar spin ephemeris model. The remaining differences form the timing residuals (δt):

  • White Noise: Radiometer noise, pulse jitter, and calibration errors scaled per observing epoch.
  • Red Noise: Intrinsic spin noise, rotational irregularities, and rotational glitches modeled via power spectral density filters.
  • Common-Mode Signal: The target stochastic gravitational wave background signature correlated across multiple sightlines.

3. Hellings-Downs Spatial Correlation

The hallmark proof of a stochastic gravitational wave background is the verification of the Hellings-Downs curve, which predicts the expected cross-correlation coefficient between pairs of pulsars as a function of their angular separation (θ) on the sky.

4. Reference Python PTA Noise Screening Snippet

import numpy as np
from enterprise.pulsar import Pulsar

def evaluate_pulsar_noise(par_file, tim_file):
    psr = Pulsar(par_file, tim_file)
    residuals = psr.residuals
    rms = np.sqrt(np.mean(residuals**2))
    print(f"Pulsar: {psr.name} | Span: {psr.span} yrs | RMS Residual: {rms*1e6:.2f} us")
