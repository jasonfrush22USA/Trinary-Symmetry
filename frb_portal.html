Fast Radio Burst (FRB) Dispersion & Scattering Explorer

1. Astrophysical Context & Overview

Fast Radio Bursts (FRBs) are millisecond-long energetic radio flashes originating from cosmological distances. As these pulses travel through ionized plasma across the universe, free electrons imprint a distinct frequency-dependent time delay known as dispersion, alongside multipath scattering tails caused by turbulent plasma structures along the line of sight.

2. The Dispersion Measure (DM) Law

The arrival time delay ($\Delta t$) between observing frequencies $f_1$ and $f_2$ is governed by the cold plasma dispersion relation:

t(f) = 4.148808 × 10^3 * DM * (f_GHz^-2)  [ms]

Where the Dispersion Measure (DM) represents the integrated column density of free electrons along the path: $\text{DM} = \int n_e \, dl \, [\text{pc cm}^{-3}]$.

3. Cosmic Probes of Intergalactic Matter

By measuring the excess DM beyond what is expected from our own Milky Way galaxy ($DM_{\text{MW}}$), astronomers can isolate the intergalactic medium component ($DM_{\text{IGM}}$). This acts as a direct cosmic weighing scale to locate the "missing baryons" of the universe.

4. Reference Python Dedispersion Screening Snippet

import numpy as np

def calculate_dispersion_delay(dm: float, freq_low_ghz: float, freq_high_ghz: float):
    k = 4.148808e3
    delay_ms = k * dm * ((freq_low_ghz**-2) - (freq_high_ghz**-2))
    return delay_ms
